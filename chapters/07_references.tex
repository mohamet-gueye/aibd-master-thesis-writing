\chapter{References}
\label{chap:references}

The reference list below follows the Harvard (author-date) convention. It contains every source cited in the body of this thesis and no others. Fourteen research publications provide the empirical, analytical, and methodological foundation used throughout the thesis; one practitioner interview provides the operational and strategic context for Blaise Diagne International Airport used in Chapters 1, 2, 5, and 6. Where an accessible version of a publication is a preprint rather than a publisher-hosted record, this is stated explicitly in the entry so that the status of the source remains transparent to the reader.

Primary research data for this thesis were obtained through the FlightRadar24 (FR24) application programming interface and are not published sources; the datasets, notebooks, simulation model files, and dashboard artifacts produced during the study are documented in Chapter 8 (Appendices) rather than listed here, since they constitute the author’s own materials rather than external literature.

\section{Notes on Source Status and Citation Practice}
\label{sec:source-status-citation-practice}

Three points regarding the composition of this reference list are recorded here for transparency.

First, two of the fifteen sources are accessible principally as preprints rather than as publisher-hosted records. \textcite{cao2025robust} is available through arXiv as a contribution to the First US-Europe Air Transportation Research and Development Symposium, and \textcite{zhu2024spatiotemporal} is available through arXiv without a corresponding journal record accessible at the time of writing. Both are cited in the thesis for their methodological approach and reported results. Both entries explicitly state their preprint status so the reader can appropriately weigh their peer-review status. This practice follows the position stated in the project’s literature review, which noted that a substantial proportion of the closest airport operations in this research niche are accessible primarily through preprint repositories.

Second, two publications by overlapping author groups are cited separately because they represent distinct studies rather than versions of a single work. \textcite{shao2019flight} is the SIGSPATIAL conference paper that introduced the airport situational-awareness map and the associated airport traffic-complexity features; \textcite{shao2022predicting} is the subsequent Neurocomputing article that develops the TrajCNN architecture on that foundation. The thesis cites both because Chapter 3 draws on the feature-engineering precedent established in the earlier work and on the spatio-temporal modeling results reported in the later one.

Third, the year assigned to two entries requires a brief note. \textcite{pang2024machine} appeared online in December 2023 but belongs to the 2024 volume of Transportation Research Part C, and is cited by its volume year throughout the thesis. \textcite{zhang2021spatiotemporal} was presented at the 2020 IPCCC conference; the accessible preprint was posted in March 2021, and the study is cited by that year in the thesis, consistent with the citation form used in the project’s literature-review documentation. In both cases, the year used in the in-text citations matches the year given in this reference list, and no source appears under more than one year.

All in-text citations in Chapters 1 to 6 were verified against this list, and all entries in this list were verified against the source documents held in the project’s reports/papers directory or, where no local copy was held, against the publisher or repository record. No entry has been included for which complete and verifiable bibliographic information could not be established.
